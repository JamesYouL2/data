Dynasty buy / sell

Paste a Sleeper league ID, pick your team, and get a buy, hold, sell or cut call for every player, for most teams (Default) or for a team that is all-in on this year or rebuilding. The calls come from backtests of FantasyPros dynasty rankings against what players went on to score.

The league ID is the long number in the league's URL on sleeper.com (or in the app under League settings). Enter a username to choose from that user's leagues.

What the calls mean
Buy: strong
Players like this returned at least 1.5 times what the average player at their price did, even without the best one, and at least 1 in 4 beat their price. Pay the market price, or a little more.
Buy: weak
Players like this returned about their price or somewhat more, or a lot more only because of one hit. Buy at the market price or below, not above.
Hold
Price and production roughly agree. Keep it unless an offer beats the price.
Hold to February
Rookies whose price tends to rise through the offseason.
Use, then sell
A starter whose long-term value is fading: play him through your playoffs, then sell.
Sell: heavy
Players like this returned under 65% of what the average player at their price did over three years (points above replacement plus what they were worth then). Take any reasonable offer, even below market.
Sell: light
Players like this returned somewhat less than their price, or about the same. Sell at the market price. If no one pays it, holding costs little.
Sell high now
Rookie RBs whose price peaks in November. Move them while it's worth something.
Lottery ticket
Cheap, mostly returns nothing, occasionally hits. Keep if you have the roster spot.
Stash
Out for the season (injured and off the FantasyPros rest-of-season rankings) but worth keeping: a contender can't use him this year, and selling now takes an injury discount.
Buy low (injured)
Out for the season, and the trade market has marked him well below his dynasty ranking. Contenders are the natural sellers.
Sell when healthy
The long-term call is to sell, but he's out for the season: wait until he's playing and the injury discount is gone.
Cut
Players like this returned close to nothing. Free the roster spot.

Strong, weak, heavy, light. Each call's note says what players like it, at the same price (FantasyPros rank 1-50, 51-100, 101-200 or 201+), returned against the average player at that price (top 340, 2018 on). Buys are judged by what each mode plays for: Rebuilding by three seasons of points plus what the player was worth after them, Default by next season plus his value a year on, Contending by next season's points. A buy that didn't pay off by that measure becomes Hold. Sells use the three-year measure in every mode. Small groups borrow the record of similar players (same position and age, then any price, then same age), and the note says so.

Default, Contending, Rebuilding. Rebuilding is the long-term call: sell what loses value, even if he starts for you. Default keeps a player while he's starting for you (top 12 QB, or 24 in superflex; top 24 RB, 30 WR, 12 TE by points per game, or by the FantasyPros rest-of-season rank) and sells him once he isn't. Where rebuilders sell and contenders buy, it holds. Contending is Default plus the contender's buys. In a league simulation (12 teams, real scoring 2019-2025), Default and Contending won more titles over the next three seasons than the old contender calls, which held fading players who weren't starting. Contending won the most titles in season 1; Default won about as many over three seasons and kept far more dynasty value. Rebuilding kept the most dynasty value. That makes it the call for a team playing for year four and later.

Price is the FantasyPros dynasty rank the calls are built on. Market is the FantasyCalc rank from real trades, with the rookie pick of similar value; ▲/▼ marks a 10%+ move in 30 days. A note under a call says when the market is well above or below the rankings (by 12+ spots, or 25%). Now shows the FantasyPros rest-of-season (ROS) positional and overall rank, and next week's positional rank, opponent and projected PPR points.
